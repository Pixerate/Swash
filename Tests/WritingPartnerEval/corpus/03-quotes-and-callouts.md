# Quotes and Callouts in Technical Writing

When you're documenting a system, a well-placed quote or callout draws the reader's eye to the one rule they mustn't miss. I've seen long manuals where critical warnings were buried in the middle of a dense paragraph, and people skipped right past them.

> Simplicity is about subtracting the obvious and adding the meaningful.
> When in doubt, leave the extra chrome out of the window.

I agree with that advice, and I try to follow it whenever we add a new preference pane. It's easy to add another toggle, but it's much harder to design a default that works out of the box.

> [!NOTE]
> The editor stores offsets in raw Markdown coordinates so attachments don't shift the selection.

> [!TIP]
> Press the undo shortcut right after a punctuation fix if you'd rather keep the original wording.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Security-scoped bookmarks must be saved before the document window closes, or the app can't load sibling images on the next launch.

> [!WARNING]
> Don't modify the text storage directly during an active Writing Tools session, because the system animation owns those attribute runs.

Why do callouts work better than bold warnings in every paragraph? They give the page clear visual landmarks without shouting at the reader on every line. I'll keep using them for real pitfalls, and I'd avoid stacking two banners back to back when a plain sentence is OK.
